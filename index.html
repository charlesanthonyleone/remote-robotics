00 // MISSION

Reach without risk

DIST: UNLIMITED
The most valuable terrain on Earth — and above it — is the terrain that kills people. We build the machines that go instead.

Every frontier has a body count. Crevasse fields swallow survey teams. Pressure at the trench floor crushes hulls. Canyon walls don't forgive a single bad hold. For a century the price of knowing what's out there has been paid in human risk.

Remote Robotics changes the math. Our machines are expendable by design and human by intent: rugged, field-serviceable robots driven by skilled remote operators who bring judgment, curiosity and instinct to places their bodies never have to go.

PRINCIPLE 01

Expendable by design

Machines are built to be lost. Modular, cheap to replace, engineered for the mission — not for a museum.

PRINCIPLE 02

Human in the loop

Piloted, not autonomous. The operator is the intelligence; the robot is the reach.

PRINCIPLE 03

A fleet, not a gadget

Climbers, mules, telepresence rigs and fabrication swarms designed to support one another in the field.

Arachne Series

STAGE: PROTOTYPE PROGRAM
RR-A // EIGHT-LIMB CLIMBING PLATFORM

ARACHNE

PROTOTYPE PROGRAM

Arachne is a human-piloted, eight-limbed climbing robot for vertical and broken terrain — rock walls, ice faces, collapsed structures, the seams of the planet where wheels and boots fail. Every limb ends in an articulated claw. Every chassis carries sealed storage ports for samples and payload.

It is built to be expendable. When terrain is so hostile that losing anything inside it is expected, Arachne is what you lose — not a person.

VARIANT A-C

ARACHNE // CANYON

Rock-climbing specialist for slot canyons, cliff bands and boulder chokes.

  • Independently movable camera masts — look around a ledge before committing a limb
  • UNSTUCK system: contracts its stance to slip out of a wedge, or runs a high-frequency shake cycle to break free of rubble and sand
  • Hardened claws for friction holds on sandstone, limestone and granite
  • Storage ports for rock and mineral samples
VARIANT A-G

ARACHNE // GLACIER

Ice-climbing specialist for glaciers and crevasse systems no human team can safely enter.

  • Thermal sensors read ice stability ~40 cm into the surface before every move (design target)
  • Descends into crevasses to inspect what is frozen deep in the cracks
  • Claws and coring tools capture ice and meltwater samples into sealed ports
  • Cold-soaked power and actuator design for polar deployment

Destrier

STAGE: IN DEVELOPMENT
RR-D // LEGGED RESUPPLY MULE

DESTRIER

IN DEVELOPMENT

A destrier was the medieval warhorse — the animal you trusted with the most important load. Ours is a four-legged robotic pack mule that walks charging equipment, spare modules and supplies into terrain too remote, too rough or too socked-in for an airdrop to be enough.

Destrier is the fleet's logistics backbone. It carries the recharging rigs that keep Arachne climbers in the field, swaps batteries, hauls samples out, and walks where tracks bog down and rotors can't land.

// Lineage: 1893

On February 14, 1893, the U.S. Patent Office granted Patent No. 491,927, "Mechanical Horse," to Lewis A. Rygg of St. Paul, Minnesota (filed April 1892). Rygg's horse was rider-powered: stirrup pedals drove a gear train and cranks that walked all four legs, with stop-jointed knees, rubber-soled hoofs and a steerable head and forelegs. Destrier is that idea with 130 years of motors, sensors and batteries behind it.

Image: Patent case file cover, U.S. National Archives via DPLA / Wikimedia Commons, public domain.

Deeplink

STAGE: IN DEVELOPMENT
RR-V // VR TELEPRESENCE PILOTING

DEEPLINK

IN DEVELOPMENT

Deeplink is the piloting layer for the whole fleet: headset, haptic suit, and a low-latency link to a machine somewhere no person should be.

In orbit: astronauts operate robots outside the station instead of suiting up for every risky task — the machine goes out the airlock, the human stays inside.

On the deep ocean floor: purpose-built seabed robots that operators can lease machine time on — survey, sample and document, and earn by the value of what they discover, not the hours they log.

03.1 // DOCTRINE

Why piloted — not autonomous

CORE THESIS
01

Unknown terrain

Every autonomous system is trained on what has already been seen. The trench floor, inner glacier systems and unmapped canyon passages have not been seen.

02

No training set

This terrain demands flexible, curiosity-driven intelligence that no dataset has captured — because the dataset doesn't exist yet.

03

Intuition finds it

The hunch that something matters when the sensors show nothing remarkable. The detail that doesn't fit. These aren't inefficiencies — they're the source of discovery.

04

Pilots train the future

Every operator session generates the training data that will let the machines work more independently over time.

The machine is the reach. The human is the reason the reach produces anything worth having.

Hiveforge

STAGE: CONCEPT STUDY
RR-H // SWARM FABRICATION

HIVEFORGE

CONCEPT STUDY

Hiveforge turns a coordinated swarm of drones into a single, building-sized 3D printer. No gantry, no crane, no fixed footprint — the printer is the swarm, and it goes wherever the swarm can fly.

Two build modes share one coordination brain:

MODE // MASON

Drones pick, place and bond bricks or blocks course by course, each unit placing in sequence from a shared build plan.

MODE // EXTRUDE

Drones melt and deposit material — polymer or cementitious mix — layer by layer, collectively, exactly like a printer head, but distributed.
